Holiday RPG Dice Buying Guide for Game Stores: Gift Sets, Assortments & Reorders

Build a useful holiday dice assortment without guessing at demand: match products to gift shoppers, make every set easy to understand, and base reorders on your own sales and supplier timing.

By DND Custom Dice Editorial TeamPublished October 8, 2026About 12 minutes

Scope: This is an independent buying guide, not a sales forecast or a promise of holiday delivery. Product availability, MOQ, production schedules, freight, and customs timing vary by SKU, destination, and order. Confirm current terms with each supplier before committing.

Holiday shoppers often arrive with a simple question: “What would make a good gift for someone who plays tabletop RPGs?” A game store can answer that question better when its holiday RPG dice assortment is organized by use and shopper confidence, not just by color. A complete, clearly labeled set is easier to buy for a new player; a theme-led set can suit someone who already plays; and a more distinctive material or presentation can serve a collector when the store can explain its care and handling.

This guide is written for independent game stores planning RPG dice gifts and seasonal stock. It offers a practical method for selecting wholesale dice, writing shelf information, estimating replenishment needs, and protecting cash from guesses that look like forecasts. It does not prescribe a universal number of units. Your own sales history, local customer base, open-to-buy budget, and supplier lead times are better inputs than a generic holiday quantity.

Quick plan: choose one complete-set option that is easy to understand, add a focused range of theme-led sets, test a small premium tier only if your customers support it, and make each package’s contents and care needs obvious. Set an inventory review date now, verify supplier timelines in writing, and reorder from SKU-level sales rather than from impressions.

The 30-minute holiday dice buying plan

  1. Export or write down the last comparable weeks of dice sales by SKU. Separate full polyhedral sets from loose dice, accessories, and premium products.
  2. Mark each current SKU as core play, giftable theme, premium/collector, or add-on. If two items do the same job, decide whether both earn shelf space.
  3. Check sellable on-hand units, confirmed purchase orders, landed cost, gross margin, shelf space, and any returns or damage.
  4. Review the product samples and package information before selecting a gift assortment. Record exact dice count, material, size, and care instructions.
  5. Work backward from the date you need stock on the shelf, allowing time for samples or artwork, production, freight, customs, receiving, and a buffer.
  6. Schedule at least one stock review before the rush and one review after it, with the sales and stock data you will use already defined.

Start with the person buying the gift

Gift shoppers are not one audience. Some know the recipient’s game and character; others only know that the person plays D&D or another tabletop RPG. A good retail assortment helps both groups make a confident choice. For each product, write down the likely shopper, the reason it belongs in the assortment, and the one question staff should ask before recommending it.

The new or occasional player

Make a complete, versatile polyhedral set the easiest product to understand. Show the number of pieces and identify the dice included. A common seven-piece set contains a d4, d6, d8, d10, percentile d10, d12, and d20, but packaging can vary. Link customers who want a closer explanation to our guide on how many dice are in a D&D dice set. Avoid assuming a gift buyer knows what a percentile die is or whether a pouch is included.

The regular player choosing for a character or campaign

Offer a small number of distinct visual themes rather than a wall of near-duplicates. Color, inclusions, engraved details, or a recognizable fantasy motif can help a player connect dice with a character concept. Our fantasy RPG dice themes page shows the kind of theme categories a buyer might compare; use the designs that fit your customer base and catalog availability.

The collector or premium-gift shopper

Premium products need useful explanations, not just a higher shelf position. State what the set is made from, how it feels or handles, what the package includes, and whether a tray or padded storage is recommended. Metal dice, for example, are heavier than common resin sets and may need a suitable rolling surface. Avoid vague claims such as “best quality” unless you can explain the specific product attribute behind them.

The Dungeon Master, host, or group buyer

For someone buying for a table or a game-night host, consider practical additions: an extra complete set for a new player, a clearly identified spare d20, a dice bag, or a tray if those products are part of your range. Keep accessories as optional complements. Do not let bundles obscure what is included or make the buyer pay for items they do not need.

Build a focused holiday RPG dice assortment

A useful holiday dice assortment has roles, not a fixed SKU count. Start with what already sells and what customers ask for; then identify a gap. A simple role map helps a buyer explain why each item is on the shelf and prevents the seasonal order from becoming a collection of attractive but interchangeable products.

  • Core gift: complete, readable polyhedral sets with clear contents and accessible packaging. These answer the “I need a safe, useful gift” question.
  • Theme-led choice: a few contrasting colors or fantasy themes for shoppers who know the recipient’s taste. Keep each design visually distinct enough to justify its place.
  • Premium test: a limited selection of products with a specific material, finish, or presentation difference. Confirm care needs and margin before buying deeper.
  • Small add-on: compatible storage, a tray, a single-die option, or another relevant accessory only when your store has evidence customers want it.

This is a planning framework, not a forecast. If your store primarily serves beginners, the core gift role may deserve more attention. A collector-heavy store may choose a wider premium test. If your cash budget is tight, prioritize replenishable items with observed demand instead of trying to represent every material. Compare the framework with your current wholesale dice catalog options and your own point-of-sale report.

Use a worksheet instead of guessing at unit quantities

Before submitting a wholesale dice order, make one row per SKU and fill in the following fields. This takes more work than choosing an arbitrary “holiday quantity,” but it makes tradeoffs visible and gives you a reason for every line on the purchase order.

  • SKU and product role: core gift, theme, premium test, or add-on.
  • Recent units sold in a comparable period, plus the date range and any promotion or event that may have affected sales.
  • Current sellable stock, units on confirmed order, damaged/held units, and the quantity reserved for online or event sales.
  • Wholesale cost, inbound freight and duties where known, retail price, and gross margin after landed cost.
  • Supplier lead time, reorder cutoff, case pack or MOQ, package dimensions, and whether the design is standard stock or made to order.
  • Review date, desired minimum stock, and the decision owner who can approve a reorder.
Simple reorder trigger: estimate expected sales during the confirmed replenishment lead time, add the safety buffer your store chooses, then subtract sellable stock and confirmed incoming units. Treat the result as a review signal, not an automatic purchase order. Check margin, cash, remaining selling days, and supplier availability before reordering.

For a seasonal item, “expected sales” should come from your own SKU history or a clearly labeled test assumption. If you have no comparable history, place a small test only if the MOQ and cash exposure are acceptable, then review early enough to act. If a supplier’s minimum order would leave you with too much stock after the season, ask about an existing design, mixed assortment, or a smaller sample before committing. Check the dice as a product and a gift

Holiday buying is compressed: shoppers may have little time to compare alternatives, and gift recipients may not be able to exchange a product easily. A quick inspection process reduces avoidable confusion. Review a physical sample wherever possible; photos alone do not reveal all details of finish, scale, weight, or package fit. Our dice sample kit checklist provides a more detailed inspection sequence.

  • Completeness: count the dice and compare them with the package list. If the set is not a standard seven-piece set, name the actual contents.
  • Readability: look at the numerals under ordinary store lighting, from a normal viewing distance. Check contrast on more than one face and turn the die to see whether decorative elements obscure the number.
  • Finish and consistency: compare multiple units from a sample lot for visible defects, sharp edges where unintended, bubbles or inclusions, inking, and variation. Define what is acceptable with the supplier; do not infer production consistency from one sample.
  • Use and care: check whether the material or shape needs a rolling tray, gentle handling, or particular storage. Put concise, accurate care information where the recipient can find it.
  • Identification: confirm that staff can distinguish the SKU, material, package contents, and reorder code without opening every retail unit.

For a resin assortment, compare material options and sample criteria in our wholesale resin dice guide for game stores. For metal products, add weight, finish, and storage to the inspection record. Do not promise that a sample proves every production unit will be identical; instead, ask how the supplier handles approved samples, tolerances, and quality checks.

Make each dice gift set easy to buy and easy to take home

Packaging has two jobs at retail: protect the dice and help the shopper understand the gift. A closed box can look polished but still leave a buyer unsure whether it includes a complete set, a pouch, or a tray. Use packaging copy, an insert, a product card, or a shelf label to make the important details visible without opening a sealed unit.

Check that the package is sized for the dice and any accessories, closes securely, and survives the normal handling expected on your shelves and during take-home transport. If you plan to wrap it, confirm that the outer box is presentable without additional work. If the package is not specifically gift-ready, do not label it that way; offer store wrapping only if the store can reliably provide it.

For private-label or custom work, review the logo position, color proof, text accuracy, barcode space, insert, and package dimensions before approval. Confirm whether a packaging MOQ applies separately from the dice MOQ, and whether a sample is a digital mockup or a physical proof. Our wholesale DND dice packaging guide covers bags, boxes, tins, inserts, and private-label questions. Seasonal customization should not proceed until the full schedule and cost are understood.

Work backward from the date stock must be ready

“Ships in a few weeks” is not the same as “ready to sell in my store.” The end-to-end timeline can include selecting a SKU, ordering and evaluating a sample, approving artwork, production, pack-out, international freight, customs clearance, delivery to your location, receiving inspection, labeling, and shelf setup. Standard catalog inventory and custom dice or packaging can follow different schedules. Destination, carrier, season, and order details can change the estimate.

Write down the date you need the products available for sale. Ask the supplier for separate estimates for sample, production, and transit, and ask what could delay each stage. Include receiving time and a contingency buffer that matches your business. Get the current MOQ, payment schedule, shipping terms, carton details, and delivery estimate in writing. If the timeline is too tight, use available stock or defer the custom launch instead of advertising an unconfirmed date.

Faire’s inventory planning guide recommends factoring seasonality and vendor lead times into buying decisions, and its calendar notes holiday buying activity from July through November. That is general retailer guidance, not a dice-specific deadline; use it as a reminder to plan ahead and verify your own vendor’s schedule. Shopify’s current busy-season checklist also emphasizes accurate inventory records, staff readiness, and preparing for post-season returns. For dice stores, those ideas translate into a SKU count, a short staff product brief, and a written after-holiday review plan.

Use labels that help an unfamiliar gift buyer

Do not make a gift shopper decode a shelf full of product names. A concise label can answer four questions: What is it? How many dice are included? What is distinctive about it? What does the box contain? Keep technical terms such as “percentile d10” accurate but explain them in plain language when space allows. Where products differ by material or care, make the difference visible before checkout.

A practical shelf card might follow this format: “Complete 7-piece polyhedral set — resin — includes d4, d6, d8, d10, percentile d10, d12 and d20 — pouch included.” Only use the wording if it matches the actual product. A theme-led product can add one short design cue, while a premium set should state the material and any handling note. Avoid claims such as “balanced,” “collector grade,” or “limited” unless you can substantiate them for that SKU.

Ask staff to use one simple question: “Do they need a complete set for play, or do they already collect dice and prefer a particular style?” That helps distinguish a functional first set from a theme or premium gift. If the shopper does not know, point to a clearly labeled, versatile option instead of pushing an expensive set.

Make reorders from evidence, not holiday excitement

Set a recurring review before the season begins. For each SKU, check sales since the previous review, sellable units, confirmed inbound stock, supplier lead time, gross margin after landed cost, and how many selling days remain. A fast-selling item may merit replenishment, but only if it can arrive in time and the order does not tie up more cash than the store can afford. A slow item may need better product information or placement, but changing the shelf card does not guarantee a sale.

Separate the holiday effect from regular demand where possible. Record promotions, local events, new game releases, and online sales that may distort the comparison. After the season, identify what to keep year-round, what to reorder next year, what needs a different package or description, and what should be discontinued. Track sell-through and margin, not just revenue; include returns, damage, markdowns, and remaining stock in the review.

Questions to send a wholesale dice supplier

Use a short, specific message so the reply can be compared across suppliers. Include the destination and the date the stock must be ready. Ask:

  • Which selected SKUs are currently available, and which are made to order?
  • What is the MOQ by dice design, material, and packaging? Can any mixed assortment be approved?
  • Can you provide a physical sample, and what features of the sample should be treated as representative?
  • What are the current production, packing, shipping, and destination delivery estimates?
  • What are the package dimensions, carton quantities, product identifiers, and exact set contents?
  • Which costs are included in the quote, and which freight, duty, or import charges are estimated separately?
  • For custom packaging, when will artwork and a physical proof need approval to meet the requested ship date?

Quick Answers

Holiday RPG dice buying FAQ

What RPG dice should a game store stock for holiday gift shoppers?

Start with complete, clearly labeled polyhedral sets that are easy to understand as gifts. Add a few theme-led and premium options only when they fit your customers, cash plan, and ability to explain material, care, and package contents.

How many dice are in a standard D&D dice set?

A common seven-piece polyhedral set contains a d4, d6, d8, d10, percentile d10, d12, and d20. Package contents vary, so verify and label each SKU rather than assume every set is identical.

How much wholesale RPG dice inventory should a store order?

Use store-specific sales, current sellable stock, confirmed incoming units, supplier lead time, and a safety buffer. There is no universal order quantity that fits every store, assortment, or holiday calendar.

Are custom dice orders suitable for a holiday launch?

They may be, but only when artwork approval, sample production, MOQ, manufacturing, packaging, freight, customs, and receiving time are confirmed in writing with enough schedule buffer. Do not advertise an arrival date based on an unverified estimate.

What should a retailer check on a dice gift set?

Check exact contents, number readability, finish consistency, package protection, shelf dimensions, material and care information, barcode or SKU identification, and whether the packaging is genuinely gift-ready.

When should a game store reorder holiday dice?

Set a review date before the seasonal rush, then compare sales velocity and remaining stock with supplier lead time. Reorder only when observed demand, margin, delivery timing, and remaining selling days justify it.
