Custom Dice Buying Guide

Custom D20 dice: logo, symbol, size and MOQ guide.

A custom d20 can be a brand mark, campaign reward, class token, retail upgrade, or collector product. The best result comes from choosing a readable symbol, practical size, suitable material, and realistic MOQ before sampling.

By DND Custom Dice Editorial TeamUpdated July 28, 2026About 8 minutes

For B2B buyers: This guide is written for RPG publishers, Kickstarter creators, dice brands, distributors, game stores, and event teams sourcing custom d20 dice or custom polyhedral dice sets. Pricing is usually quote-based because material, quantity, logo method, packaging, and shipping assumptions change the final cost.

The d20 is the most recognizable die in tabletop RPG play. It handles attacks, checks, saving throws, dramatic failures, and high-value moments at the table. That is why many buyers choose the d20 as the first place to add a custom logo, campaign symbol, faction mark, dragon eye, class icon, or publisher brand.

Good custom d20 dice are not only attractive. They must be readable, balanced enough for normal tabletop use, consistent across bulk production, easy to package, and clear enough for buyers to understand from photos. A small mistake on the custom face can make the whole set feel cheap. A careful specification can make a simple d20 feel like a premium RPG product.

Custom d20 dice: the quick answer

For most wholesale or crowdfunding projects, the safest custom d20 plan is a standard-size d20 inside a 7-piece polyhedral set, with one clean logo or symbol replacing the 20 face. Use high-contrast color fill, avoid tiny lines, approve a physical sample, and choose packaging before quoting bulk production.

If the d20 is a standalone product, collector reward, or event giveaway, consider oversized d20 dice or premium material such as metal, sharp edge resin, or liquid core. Just remember that larger dice affect tooling, weight, packaging, freight, and MOQ. Treat oversized d20 dice as a separate product, not a small variation of a standard set.

1. Decide why the buyer needs a custom d20

The best specification depends on the commercial use case. A game store display d20, a Kickstarter stretch goal, a publisher logo die, and a private label collector product should not be quoted in the same way.

This first decision prevents wasted design time. A playable d20 should prioritize comfort and number visibility. A display d20 can prioritize size and visual impact. A retail d20 needs packaging and SKU clarity. A campaign d20 needs reward-tier math and delivery timing.

2. Keep the logo or symbol face simple

The most common request is a custom logo on the 20 face. This works well when the logo is bold, centered, and not too detailed. Thin lines, small text, complex gradients, and miniature illustrations often lose clarity after engraving, printing, painting, or color filling. If the mark is too complex, simplify it into an icon version before production.

For RPG projects, useful symbol directions include dragon eyes, spell circles, guild marks, class icons, faction emblems, skulls, moons, swords, shields, runes, and campaign crests. The symbol should still read at tabletop distance. A buyer should be able to understand the custom face from a product photo without zooming in.

Send artwork as vector files when possible. AI, EPS, SVG, or clean PDF files are better than screenshots. If only a PNG is available, send the largest clean version and explain whether the supplier should treat it as reference art or final production artwork.

3. Choose the right d20 size

Standard d20 dice are the practical choice for most RPG sets because they fit normal dice trays, retail boxes, dice bags, and fulfillment cartons. They also match buyer expectations when sold as part of a 7-piece polyhedral dice set.

Oversized d20 dice create stronger visual impact. They are useful for collector products, GM gifts, convention giveaways, display pieces, milestone rewards, and limited-edition campaign tiers. However, oversized d20 dice need more material, stronger packaging, and more shipping space. If the project involves 35mm, 45mm, 55mm, or larger d20 dice, confirm the packaging and freight assumptions early.

4. Match material to the selling position

Resin d20 dice are flexible for color themes, inclusions, swirls, translucent effects, dragon concepts, elemental designs, and fantasy campaign colors. They are often a practical starting point for custom RPG dice sets because they can look distinctive without making the product too heavy.

Sharp edge resin d20 dice feel more premium and photograph well, especially for collector rewards and boutique dice brands. They need careful sample approval for edge finish, polish quality, bubble control, number depth, and color fill.

Metal d20 dice work well for premium gift sets, dragon themes, dark fantasy lines, faction coins, and high-value retail products. They feel substantial, but the weight affects packaging and shipping. Hollow metal d20 dice can reduce weight while keeping a premium metal appearance, depending on the design.

Liquid core and specialty d20 dice are best used when the moving center, visual depth, or novelty effect is part of the selling story. They can attract attention, but they require more sample review because buyers need to confirm the movement effect, clarity, and number readability.

5. Understand what changes MOQ

MOQ is not one fixed number for every custom d20 dice project. It depends on material, tooling, logo method, color fill, finish, packaging, and whether the order is d20-only or part of a full set. A custom d20 face inside an existing production style may be easier than a fully new shape, color system, and custom packaging project.

Packaging may have its own MOQ. A printed retail box, magnetic gift box, insert card, belly band, barcode label, or display carton can require separate production quantities. This is why a buyer should discuss dice MOQ and packaging MOQ together. If the project is for Kickstarter, give the supplier a base case and stretch case so the quote can support different pledge outcomes.

6. Approve samples before bulk production

A custom d20 sample should be checked for logo clarity, number readability, color fill consistency, surface finish, weight, and overall feel. Do not approve a design only because the digital mockup looks good. Real material behaves differently under light, especially translucent resin, glitter resin, metallic finishes, dark number fills, and detailed logo faces.

For bulk production, keep a record of the approved sample photos, logo file, Pantone or color reference if available, number fill color, packaging artwork, and final quantity. This prevents confusion when reordering or expanding the product into a wider custom dice series.

7. Choose packaging before final quote approval

Packaging can change the perceived value more than buyers expect. A single d20 in a small bag feels like a giveaway. A custom d20 in a tin, acrylic display box, magnetic box, or branded retail box feels like a finished product. For a full RPG dice set, the d20 may be the hero face in the product photography, while the packaging carries the brand story.

For retail buyers and dice brands, packaging should support SKU identification, barcode placement, product naming, warning labels when needed, and shelf display. For crowdfunding creators, packaging should support fulfillment speed and protect the dice during international shipping. For events, packaging should be simple enough to distribute quickly.

8. Send a complete custom d20 quote checklist

A complete inquiry saves time and usually produces a more accurate quote. Instead of asking only for the price of custom d20 dice, send the full context.

  • Buyer type: RPG publisher, Kickstarter creator, game store, dice brand, distributor, event team, or subscription box.
  • Product format: single custom d20, oversized d20, full 7-piece set, d20 pair, or mixed dice assortment.
  • Material preference: resin, sharp edge resin, metal, hollow metal, liquid core, or catalog-based style.
  • Logo position: 20 face, 1 face, packaging only, or dice and packaging together.
  • Artwork files: vector logo, symbol reference, color palette, number fill color, and packaging design if available.
  • Quantity target: sample kit, 100 pieces, 300 pieces, 1,000+ pieces, or campaign-dependent estimate.
  • Packaging format: bag, tin, display box, magnetic gift box, printed retail box, or private label carton.
  • Delivery information: destination country, target launch date, target delivery date, and freight preference.

When these details are clear, the supplier can respond with practical options instead of a vague range. The buyer can then compare product quality, MOQ, packaging, and timeline on the same basis.

Quick Answers

Custom d20 dice FAQ

Can I put my logo on the d20 face?

Yes. A logo or symbol on the 20 face is common for custom d20 dice. The artwork should be clean, centered, and simplified enough to remain readable after engraving, printing, painting, or color filling.

Is custom d20-only production better than a full dice set?

It depends on the use case. A d20-only product can work for events, promotions, and collector gifts. A full 7-piece polyhedral dice set usually works better for RPG retail, Kickstarter rewards, and private label dice lines.

What is the best material for custom d20 dice?

Resin is flexible for themed colors and broad buyer appeal. Sharp edge resin is stronger for premium presentation. Metal is suitable for high-value gifts and collector products. Liquid core and specialty styles work when the visual effect is part of the product story.

Why does packaging affect MOQ?

Printed boxes, inserts, labels, tins, and display cartons may have their own production requirements. A buyer should confirm dice MOQ and packaging MOQ together before approving a campaign reward or retail launch.

What should I check in a custom d20 sample?

Check logo clarity, number readability, color fill, surface finish, edge quality, weight, packaging fit, and photo appearance under normal light. Keep sample approval records for future bulk production and reorders.

Final recommendation

Start with the buyer use case, not the decoration. Decide whether the custom d20 is playable, promotional, collectible, or retail-ready. Then choose a simple symbol, suitable size, practical material, and packaging that fits the order quantity. A clear brief and approved sample will do more for the final product than an overcomplicated logo face.
